Prospective students & postdocs

Join the group

I'm building a new research group at Lehigh University focused on earthquakes, plate tectonics, geodesy, geomorphology, and computational Earth science. I'm always interested in working with students who are curious, motivated, and excited to tackle challenging scientific problems, regardless of their prior experience.

Current openings

Who I'm recruiting

Ph.D.

Doctoral students

I expect to recruit a Ph.D. student during the upcoming admissions cycle. Potential research projects include geodesy, geomorphology, and earthquake-sequence simulations. Ph.D. students in Lehigh's Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences are supported through a combination of research and teaching assistantships.

M.S.

Master's students

Master's projects are tailored to each student's interests and may focus on geodesy, landscape inversion, or earthquake-sequence simulations.

Undergraduate

Lehigh undergraduates

There is room for undergraduate research in the group, especially for students who enjoy computational tools. Students interested in committing to a year-long research project are particularly encouraged to get in touch.

Postdoctoral

Postdoctoral researchers

I am always interested in hearing from outstanding postdoctoral candidates. If you have your own fellowship or would like to develop a proposal together, please get in touch early to discuss potential research directions and funding opportunities.

Contact

How to Get in Touch

If you're interested in joining the group, feel free to email me at bar.oryan@lehigh.edu. In your message, it is helpful to briefly describe your background, your research interests, and what you are looking for (undergraduate research, M.S., Ph.D., or postdoctoral opportunities). Attaching a CV is always appreciated.

Formal applications are submitted through the Lehigh University Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences. If you're considering applying, I'm happy to discuss potential research projects and answer questions before the application process.

Life at Lehigh

The Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences at Lehigh University is a collaborative and interdisciplinary community with expertise spanning geology, geophysics, geomorphology, geochemistry, environmental science, and data science. Students have opportunities to work closely with faculty across disciplines while benefiting from a supportive and growing research environment.

Lehigh University is located in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, in the scenic Lehigh Valley, about 90 minutes from both New York City and Philadelphia. The region offers an excellent quality of life, with easy access to the Appalachian Mountains and a wide range of outdoor activities, while remaining close to two major metropolitan areas.
