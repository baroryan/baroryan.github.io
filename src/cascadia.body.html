A snippet · Cascadia, 26 January 1700

The forest that drowned

A magnitude 9 rupture offshore dropped this coast about a metre in minutes. Salt water reached the roots and killed the forest where it stood. Tap the markers.

How a forest becomes a ghost

Drop, drown, decay, recover — then the next one. The recovery is interseismic uplift plus tidal mud, which is why spruce are growing on ground that drowned in 1700.

The last ring is 1699

A slow drowning thins the rings for years first. These stay wide right to the outermost one and then stop — an abrupt drop, in the winter of 1699–1700. Six trees along 100 km of coast gave the same year. Schematic, not a scan.

The orphan tsunami

Nobody on this coast wrote the earthquake down. Japan wrote down the wave: village and castle records describe flooding on 26 January 1700 with no earthquake felt beforehand — an orphan tsunami, no local parent.

Match that date to the tree rings and the sand sheets left inland, and the parent is Cascadia, at about magnitude 9. Recurrence here is centuries, so the instrumental record is far too short to contain one. The landscape is the only archive long enough — which is why the geodesy and the geology in my research have to be read together.
